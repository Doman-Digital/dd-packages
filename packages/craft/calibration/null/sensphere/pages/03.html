What is OT?

Occupational therapy is about the everyday things that matter

Getting dressed, holding a pencil, sitting still at the dinner table, coping with a noisy classroom — these are the "occupations" of childhood. When a child struggles with them, it can affect confidence, friendships and learning.

Our paediatric occupational therapists assess how a child's physical, sensory and cognitive skills affect their daily life, then build a therapy plan around real routines — not just a clinic room.

Our Services

Assessment and therapy tailored to your child

Every child is different, so every plan is too. Here's how we typically support the young people we work with.

OT Assessments

Comprehensive assessments covering sensory processing, motor skills, self-care and school readiness, with a clear written report.

1:1 Therapy Sessions

Regular, playful, goal-led sessions in clinic, at home, or via telehealth to build skills at a pace that suits your child.

Sensory Processing Support

Strategies and sensory circuits to help children who feel overwhelmed, under-stimulated, or seek constant movement.

School Consultations

On-site observations, staff training and EHCP-ready reports that help schools put reasonable adjustments in place.

Parent & Carer Coaching

Practical, jargon-free guidance so strategies that work in a session also work at the dinner table and on the school run.

Handwriting & Fine Motor Groups

Small, motivating group sessions that target pencil grip, letter formation, scissor skills and hand strength.

FAQs

Common questions from families

Do we need a diagnosis before starting OT?+

No. Many families come to us before, during, or without ever pursuing a formal diagnosis. Occupational therapy focuses on function and skills, not labels, so we can start with an assessment whenever you're ready.

Can sessions take place at school?+

Yes. We regularly work in schools alongside teachers and SENCOs, and can carry out observations, deliver 1:1 or small-group sessions, and provide staff training on-site.

Do you work with families outside the local area?+

We offer telehealth consultations and parent coaching UK-wide, alongside in-person assessments and therapy across our regional service area.

How long does a typical assessment take?+

Assessments usually take 60–90 minutes, followed by a written report within two to three weeks that sets out findings and recommended next steps.
